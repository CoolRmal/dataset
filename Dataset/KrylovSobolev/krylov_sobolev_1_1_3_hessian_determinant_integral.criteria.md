# Criteria: krylov_sobolev_1_1_3_hessian_determinant_integral

**Statement:** [krylov_sobolev_1_1_3_hessian_determinant_integral.md](krylov_sobolev_1_1_3_hessian_determinant_integral.md) · **Lean:** [krylov_sobolev_1_1_3_hessian_determinant_integral.lean](krylov_sobolev_1_1_3_hessian_determinant_integral.lean)

A faithful formalization must say: for $d = 2$ and $u$ twice continuously differentiable with compact support, the integral over the whole plane of $\det u_{xx}$ vanishes. The exercise is a null-Lagrangian statement — $\det u_{xx}$ is a divergence — so compact support is exactly what makes it true, and it is *not* an identity about the individual entries of the Hessian. The hazards are the dimension (the exercise fixes $d = 2$; the remark that follows notes the result holds in every dimension, which is a different, stronger claim), the meaning of $\det$ (a genuine matrix determinant, not $u_{x^1x^1}u_{x^2x^2}$ alone), and compact support (for $u(x) = \|x\|^2/2$ the integrand is identically $1$).

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| 2 | Hypothesis completeness | $u \in C_0^2$ means twice continuously differentiable **and** compactly supported. | 3 | Faithful encoding | $\det u_{xx}$ is the determinant of the Hessian **matrix** $(u_{x^ix^j})_{i,j}$. For $d = 2$ this is $u_{x^1x^1}u_{x^2x^2} - u_{x^1x^2}u_{x^2x^1}$, which equals $u_{x^1x^1}u_{x^2x^2} - (u_{x^1x^2})^2$ only because $u \in C^2$ makes the mixed derivatives symmetric. | ✅ `(hessian u x).det` with `hessian u x = Matrix.of fun i j ↦ directionalDerivativeList [i, j] u x`; `Matrix.det` on `Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℝ` is the genuine determinant. ⚠️ Spelling the $2\times2$ determinant out by hand is equally faithful. ❗ Predicted error: writing the *trace* (i.e. $\Delta u$), for which the assertion is also true but trivial, or a product of Laplacians. |
| 4 | Junk values | `directionalDerivativeList [i, j] u` is `fderiv` applied twice, and `fderiv` is `0` off the differentiability locus. Under `ContDiff ℝ 2 u` all entries are the true second derivatives, the integrand is continuous with compact support, and `∫` is a genuine Lebesgue integral rather than its `0` fallback. | ✅ Safe. ❗ Predicted error: weakening `hu` to `Differentiable ℝ u`, which makes the second-order entries junk and the statement vacuously true. |
| 5 | Semantic closeness / index order | The Hessian entry order `[i, j]` versus `[j, i]` transposes the matrix. Determinants are transpose-invariant, so either convention gives the same statement here; the difference matters only for statements involving individual entries. | ✅ Immaterial for this problem, and `ContDiff ℝ 2 u` makes the matrix symmetric anyway. |
| 6 | Conclusion completeness | The conclusion is the single equation $\int_{\mathbb{R}^2}\det u_{xx}\,dx = 0$ over the *whole* plane, not over the support of $u$ or over a ball. | ✅ `∫ x, (hessian u x).det = 0` with the default `volume` on `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2)`, i.e. two-dimensional Lebesgue measure. ⚠️ Integrating over `tsupport u` gives the same number but is a less literal transcription. |
| 7 | Semantic closeness | "By using the Fourier transform" is a hint about the intended proof, not part of the assertion; a formalization must not encode it (e.g. by phrasing the conclusion in terms of $\hat u$). | ✅ The Fourier transform does not appear. ❗ Predicted error: formalizing the Plancherel identity used in the hint instead of the exercise's conclusion. |
