# Criteria: conway_V_13_3_james

**Statement:** [conway_V_13_3_james.md](conway_V_13_3_james.md) · **Lean:** [conway_V_13_3_james.lean](conway_V_13_3_james.lean)

A faithful formalization must state the *one* implication of James's theorem: if every continuous linear functional attains the supremum of its modulus on a norm-closed convex set $A$ in a Banach space, then $A$ is weakly compact. Three hypotheses (closedness, convexity, completeness) are all indispensable, and the conclusion is compactness of $A$ *itself* in the weak topology. The junk-value risk sits in the attainment hypothesis: written with `sSup` it would silently involve Mathlib's `sSup` convention (`sSup ∅ = 0`, and `sSup` of an unbounded set `= 0` in the conditionally complete order on `ℝ`), so the set-valued supremum must either be avoided or guarded.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Hypothesis completeness | All three structural hypotheses must be present: `hAclosed : IsClosed A` (norm-closed), `hAconvex : Convex ℝ A`, and `[CompleteSpace E]`. James's theorem is false without convexity (the unit sphere of a reflexive space attains everything and is not weakly compact) and is known to fail for incomplete normed spaces. | ✅ All three present. ❗ Predicted error: dropping `Convex ℝ A` or `CompleteSpace E`. |
| 2 | Junk values / faithful encoding | "$\|\langle x_0, x^*\rangle\| = \sup\{\|\langle x, x^*\rangle\| : x \in A\}$" is encoded attainment-style as `∃ x₀ ∈ A, ∀ x ∈ A, ‖φ x‖ ≤ ‖φ x₀‖`, which never forms a supremum and therefore cannot be satisfied by the `sSup = 0` junk value. A candidate writing `‖φ x₀‖ = sSup {‖φ x‖ \| x ∈ A}` makes the hypothesis spuriously satisfiable when the image is unbounded (`sSup` junks to `0`, so `φ = 0` and `x₀` arbitrary would satisfy it). | ✅ The `∃ x₀ ∈ A, ∀ x ∈ A, …` form. ❗ Predicted error: `sSup`/`iSup`-based attainment without a `BddAbove` guard. |
| 3 | Semantic closeness | Note the encoding also matches the text for $A = \emptyset$: both the book's "there is an $x_0$ in $A$" and `∃ x₀ ∈ A` fail, so the theorem is vacuous there rather than depending on a convention for $\sup \emptyset$. | ✅ Faithful degenerate behaviour, obtained for free from the attainment form. |
| 4 | Hypothesis completeness | The functional must range over the *whole* dual: `∀ φ : E →L[ℂ] ℂ`. Restricting to `‖φ‖ = 1`, or to a norming subset, changes the hypothesis (though restricting to `‖φ‖ = 1` happens to be equivalent by homogeneity). | ✅ `∀ φ : E →L[ℂ] ℂ` — Mathlib's `E →L[ℂ] ℂ` is exactly $\mathcal{X}^*$. |
| 5 | Conclusion completeness | The conclusion is that `A` is compact in the weak topology: `IsCompact (toWeakSpace ℂ E '' A)`, i.e. compactness of the transported set inside `WeakSpace ℂ E`. `IsCompact A` in `E` is norm compactness (wrong and much stronger); compactness of `closure (toWeakSpace ℂ E '' A)` is weaker and misses that the closed convex `A` is already weakly closed. | ✅ `IsCompact (toWeakSpace ℂ E '' A)`. ❗ Predicted error: norm compactness, or compactness of the weak closure. |
| 6 | Mathlib conventions | Convexity of a subset of a complex normed space is `Convex ℝ A` in Mathlib (the scalars must be a `LinearOrderedField`); `Convex ℂ A` does not typecheck. The modulus $\lvert \langle x, x^* \rangle \rvert$ of a complex scalar is `‖φ x‖ = Complex.abs (φ x)`. | ✅ Both conventions followed. ❗ Predicted error: attempting `Convex ℂ A`, or dropping the modulus and writing `φ x ≤ φ x₀` (which is not even well-formed for `ℂ`, and over `ℝ` states the different, sup-without-modulus variant of James's theorem). |
| 7 | Conclusion completeness | The theorem is a single implication; the converse (weakly compact ⟹ every functional attains its sup) is true but is *not* what V.13.3 asserts. A candidate stating an `↔` overstates the transcribed text. | ✅ One implication only. ⚠️ A candidate stating the `↔` is still true and should be flagged as over-strong rather than wrong. |
