## Statement

**4.32 Theorem (The Pontrjagin Duality Theorem).** The map $\Phi \colon G \to \widehat{\widehat{G}}$ defined by (4.29) is an isomorphism of topological groups.

**Notation.** $G$ is a locally compact abelian group and $\widehat{G}$ its dual group: the continuous homomorphisms $\xi \colon G \to \mathbb{T}$, with the topology of compact convergence, again a locally compact abelian group. The pairing is written $\langle x,\xi\rangle = \xi(x)$, and (4.29) defines $\Phi$ by $\langle \Phi(x), \xi\rangle = \langle x, \xi\rangle$ for all $\xi \in \widehat{G}$ — that is, $\Phi(x)$ is evaluation at $x$. "Isomorphism of topological groups" means a group isomorphism that is simultaneously a homeomorphism.
