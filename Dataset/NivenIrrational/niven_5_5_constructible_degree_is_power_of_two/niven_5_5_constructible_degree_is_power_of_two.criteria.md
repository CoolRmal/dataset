# Criteria: niven_5_5_constructible_degree_is_power_of_two

**Statement:** [niven_5_5_constructible_degree_is_power_of_two.md](niven_5_5_constructible_degree_is_power_of_two.md) · **Lean:** [niven_5_5_constructible_degree_is_power_of_two.lean](niven_5_5_constructible_degree_is_power_of_two.lean) · **Context:** [niven_5_5_constructible_degree_is_power_of_two.context.md](niven_5_5_constructible_degree_is_power_of_two.context.md)

## What the theorem says

Start with a segment of length $1$ and build new lengths with straightedge and compass. Algebraically
you can add, subtract, multiply, divide, and take square roots of lengths you already have. Niven's
Theorem on Geometric Constructions says every length you reach this way is an algebraic number, and
its degree over $\mathbb{Q}$ — the degree of its minimal polynomial — is $1$, $2$, $4$, $8$, and so
on: always a power of $2$. Each square root at most doubles the degree, so a tower of square roots
can only ever produce degrees that are powers of $2$. This is the theorem that rules out the three
classical constructions.

## What a correct formalization must contain

Each row is one thing the Lean statement has to say. A formalization that is missing any
row is incomplete.

| # | Requirement | Does the ground truth have it? |
|---|-------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | The hypothesis is that $x$ is a constructible length. | ✅ `hx : IsConstructible x`, for a real `x`. |
| 2 | The constructible lengths start from the rationals. | ✅ The `rat` constructor of `IsConstructible` puts every `q : ℚ` in. |
| 3 | They are closed under addition, negation, multiplication and inversion. | ✅ The `add`, `neg`, `mul`, `inv` constructors. Together with `rat` these give the field operations Niven allows. |
| 4 | They are closed under square roots, but only of non-negative constructed lengths. | ✅ `sqrt {x} : IsConstructible x → 0 ≤ x → IsConstructible (Real.sqrt x)`. The `0 ≤ x` guard is required. |
| 5 | First conclusion: $x$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$. | ✅ The left conjunct `IsAlgebraic ℚ x`. |
| 6 | Second conclusion: the degree of $x$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is a power of $2$. | ✅ `∃ k : ℕ, Module.finrank ℚ (Algebra.adjoin ℚ ({x} : Set ℝ)) = 2 ^ k`. |
| 7 | The exponent $k$ depends on the number $x$; it is chosen after $x$ is given. | ✅ `∃ k` sits inside the statement about the fixed `x`, after `x` and `hx` are bound. |
| 8 | Degree $1$ is allowed, since rationals are constructible. | ✅ `k : ℕ` ranges from $0$, and $2^0 = 1$. |
| 9 | The degree is the dimension over $\mathbb{Q}$ of the field generated by $x$. | ✅ `Module.finrank ℚ (Algebra.adjoin ℚ {x})`. For algebraic $x$ the subalgebra generated by $x$ **is** the field $\mathbb{Q}(x)$, so this is the minimal-polynomial degree; an `IntermediateField` spelling would name the same number. |

## Mistakes to check for

Each row is an error we expect models to make. A formalization that makes any of these is
wrong, even if it compiles.

| # | Mistake | Why it is wrong |
|---|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Concluding only `IsAlgebraic ℚ x`. | Drops the whole point. Algebraicity alone says nothing about which constructions are possible; the power-of-$2$ degree is what kills the cube and the angle. |
| 2 | Putting `∃ k` outside the quantifier over `x`. | Claims one exponent works for every constructible number, i.e. a uniform bound on degrees. False: $\sqrt2$ has degree $2$, $\sqrt[4]{2}$ has degree $4$, and the degrees are unbounded. |
| 3 | Stating an `↔`. | The converse is false. Having degree a power of $2$ is not enough; the number must sit in a tower of quadratic extensions. There are degree-$4$ algebraic numbers that are not constructible. |
| 4 | Leaving the square-root constructor unguarded, allowing `IsConstructible (Real.sqrt x)` for negative `x`. | Mathlib's `Real.sqrt` returns $0$ on negative inputs, so the clause would not add any strange number, but it does misstate the geometry: a compass cannot produce the square root of a negative length, and the definition would no longer say what the book says. |
| 5 | Giving the inductive definition no base case, or only `IsConstructible 0`. | Then nothing (or only $0$) is constructible and the theorem is true for uninteresting reasons. The starting data is the unit segment, and the closure of $1$ under the field operations is all of $\mathbb{Q}$. |
| 6 | Measuring the degree over `ℝ` or `ℂ` instead of `ℚ`. | Over `ℝ` every real has degree $1$, so the statement would be trivial. |
| 7 | Writing the degree as $2^{k+1}$, or requiring $k \ge 1$. | Excludes the rationals, which are constructible and have degree $1 = 2^0$. |
| 8 | Assuming `IsAlgebraic ℚ x` as a hypothesis. | It is half the conclusion, not something given. |

## Notes on the ground truth

- Mathlib has no field-theoretic notion of constructible number — `Topology/Constructible.lean` is
  about constructible *sets* — so the inductive `IsConstructible` in `Defs.lean` is genuinely new.
  It is shared by this problem and the three classical-construction problems.
- `Module.finrank` returns $0$ when the module is not finite-dimensional. That does not open a hole
  here: $0$ is not $2^k$ for any $k$, so a transcendental `x` could not satisfy the second conjunct
  by accident.
- The theorem is stated in one direction only, matching the book. The converse would need the tower
  condition and is not asserted.

### Fatal — any of these caps the total at 25

- Requirement 4 with square-root closure allowed for negative arguments.

- Requirement 7 with the exponent $k$ quantified before $x$.

### Domain-specific pitfalls for this problem

- Square-root closure is restricted to non-negative already-constructed quantities.
- The degree is the degree of the minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$, i.e. the dimension of $\mathbb{Q}(x)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
- Degree $1$ (the rationals) is included, so the exponent may be $0$.
- Algebraicity is part of the conclusion, not an assumption.
