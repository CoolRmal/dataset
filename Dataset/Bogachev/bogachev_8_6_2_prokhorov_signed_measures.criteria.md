# Criteria: bogachev_8_6_2_prokhorov_signed_measures

**Statement:** [bogachev_8_6_2_prokhorov_signed_measures.md](bogachev_8_6_2_prokhorov_signed_measures.md) · **Lean:** [bogachev_8_6_2_prokhorov_signed_measures.lean](bogachev_8_6_2_prokhorov_signed_measures.lean)

Bogachev's Theorem 8.6.2 is Prokhorov's theorem for *signed* (finite, possibly non-positive) Borel measures. Mathlib has weak convergence only for `ProbabilityMeasure`/`FiniteMeasure`, so the signed case needs custom definitions; the quality question is whether those definitions faithfully encode weak convergence against bounded continuous functions, uniform tightness via total variations, and the variation-norm bound — and whether both halves of the theorem (separable case; tight-family case for general complete metric spaces) survive.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Faithful encoding | The family consists of signed measures. Restricting to `Measure`/`ProbabilityMeasure` (where Mathlib has off-the-shelf weak convergence) silently proves a much weaker theorem — for probability measures the variation-bound condition is vacuous. | ✅ `S : Set (SignedMeasure X)` throughout. ❗ Trap: candidates using `ProbabilityMeasure X` and Mathlib's `Tendsto` in the weak topology. |
| 2 | Faithful encoding | The integral of `f : X →ᵇ ℝ` against a signed measure must be well-defined; the ground truth takes `∫ f d(pos part) − ∫ f d(neg part)` of the Jordan decomposition. Both parts are finite measures and `f` is bounded continuous, hence integrable — no junk values. | ✅ `signedMeasureIntegral` via `toJordanDecomposition`. |
| 3 | Semantic closeness | “Every sequence contains a weakly convergent subsequence” = relative *sequential* compactness, encoded literally: for every `s : ℕ → SignedMeasure X` with values in `S`, there is a `StrictMono φ` and a limit `t` (not required to lie in `S`!). Requiring `t ∈ S` (compactness instead of relative compactness) is a classic error. | ✅ `relatively_sequentially_weakly_compact_signed` matches; the limit is unconstrained. |
| 4 | Faithful encoding | “Uniformly tight” for signed measures means uniform tightness of the total variations. Ground truth: `IsTightMeasureSet ((fun s ↦ s.totalVariation) '' S)`, reusing Mathlib's tightness for the image family. | ✅ Faithful, and reuses `MeasureTheory.IsTightMeasureSet` rather than a hand-rolled ε–compact-set formula. |
| 5 | Hypothesis completeness | “Uniformly bounded in the variation norm” must appear as a separate condition (`∃ C, ∀ s ∈ S, s.totalVariation univ ≤ C`). For signed measures this is *not* implied by tightness; dropping it makes (ii) ⇒ (i) false. | ✅ `UniformlyBoundedInTotalVariation S`. ⚠️ An `iSup`/`sSup … < ∞` form would be semantically closer to “$\sup_\mu \|\mu\| < \infty$” than the `∃ C` form. |
| 6 | Conclusion completeness | Both parts of the theorem must be formalized: the equivalence for complete **separable** metric spaces, and the extension to arbitrary complete metric spaces when every member of `M` is tight ($M \subset \mathcal{M}_t(X)$). Models typically keep only the first. | ✅ A conjunction of two implications: one under `SecondCountableTopology X`, one under `∀ s ∈ S, IsTightMeasureSet {s.totalVariation}`. |
| 7 | Mathlib conventions | “Separable” for metric spaces is conventionally carried as `SecondCountableTopology` in Mathlib's measure-theory library (equivalent for (pseudo)metric spaces); completeness as `[CompleteSpace X]`; Borel structure as `[MeasurableSpace X] [BorelSpace X]`. | ✅ All standard typeclasses. ⚠️ `SecondCountableTopology X` appears as a hypothesis arrow rather than an instance bracket so that the two halves can share one statement — acceptable, but splitting into two theorems with `[SecondCountableTopology X]` would be more idiomatic. |
| 8 | Trap | Weak convergence must test against **bounded continuous** functions (`X →ᵇ ℝ`). Testing against compactly supported or merely continuous functions changes the topology (vague vs. weak) and the theorem. | ✅ `∀ f : X →ᵇ ℝ` in `weakly_converges_signed`. |
