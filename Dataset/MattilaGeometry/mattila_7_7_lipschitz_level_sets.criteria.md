# Criteria: mattila_7_7_lipschitz_level_sets

**Statement:** [mattila_7_7_lipschitz_level_sets.md](mattila_7_7_lipschitz_level_sets.md) · **Lean:** [mattila_7_7_lipschitz_level_sets.lean](mattila_7_7_lipschitz_level_sets.lean)

A faithful formalization must express an Eilenberg-type inequality: the integral over $y \in \mathbb{R}^m$ of $\mathcal H^{s-m}$ of the level set $A \cap f^{-1}\{y\}$ is dominated by $c(n,m)\,\mathrm{Lip}(f)^m\,\mathcal H^s(A)$, with the constant depending on the dimensions *only* — so its quantifier must precede $s$, $A$ and $f$. The delicate point is the integral itself: Mattila writes $\int^{*}$, the **upper** integral, precisely because $y \mapsto \mathcal H^{s-m}(A \cap f^{-1}\{y\})$ is not known to be measurable, and mathlib's `∫⁻` is the *lower* Lebesgue integral, so the two are not interchangeable when the integrand is non-measurable.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Faithful encoding | The textbook integral is the **upper** integral $\int^{*}$. Mathlib's `∫⁻ y, g y ∂μ` is the *lower* Lebesgue integral (the supremum over simple functions below `g`), so for a non-measurable integrand `∫⁻ ≤ ∫*` and the Lean inequality is strictly weaker than Mattila's. A literal rendering would be an infimum over measurable majorants, e.g. `⨅ g, ⨅ (_ : Measurable g), ⨅ (_ : ∀ y, F y ≤ g y), ∫⁻ y, g y ∂volume`. | ⚠️ `∫⁻ y, μH[s - m] (A ∩ f ⁻¹' {y}) ∂volume` is a genuine weakening of the stated bound (still a true statement). ❗ Predicted error: candidates will also reach for `∫⁻` — the upper-integral distinction is the substantive modelling choice here. |
| 2 | Junk values | Whatever integral is used, it must be `ℝ≥0∞`-valued: the integrand is a Hausdorff measure that is routinely `∞`, and $\mathcal H^s(A)$ may be `∞` too. A Bochner `∫` would evaluate to junk `0` exactly in the interesting cases. | ✅ `∫⁻` into `ℝ≥0∞`, with `μH[s - m]` and `μH[s]` also `ℝ≥0∞`-valued and applied to arbitrary (possibly non-measurable) sets, as outer measures. |
| 3 | Semantic closeness / quantifier order | $c(n,m)$ depends on the dimensions only: the constant must be quantified **outside** $s$, $A$, $f$ and the Lipschitz constant, but may depend on the implicit `n` and `m`. | ✅ `∃ c : ℝ≥0∞, c < ∞ ∧ ∀ (s : ℝ) (A) (f) (K : ℝ≥0), …`, with `n m` implicit in the binder list. ❗ Predicted error: `∀ A f, ∃ c, …`, which lets the constant depend on the data and makes the inequality nearly vacuous. |
| 4 | Faithful encoding | "$f : A \to \mathbb{R}^m$ Lipschitz" is a map defined *on $A$*: the relative notion `LipschitzOnWith K f A` is required. The book's $\mathrm{Lip}(f)^m$ is the least Lipschitz constant; quantifying over every `K` with `LipschitzOnWith K f A` is equivalent, since the right-hand side is monotone in `K`. | ✅ `LipschitzOnWith K f A` with `K : ℝ≥0` universally quantified. ❗ Predicted error: `LipschitzWith K f` (globally Lipschitz on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$), which is a strictly stronger hypothesis and a weaker theorem. |
| 5 | Faithful encoding | The level set is $A \cap f^{-1}\{y\}$, not $f^{-1}\{y\}$: `f` is a total Lean function but is constrained only on `A`, so its values outside `A` are junk and the intersection is what neutralizes them. | ✅ `A ∩ f ⁻¹' {y}`. ❗ Predicted error: integrating `μH[s-m] (f ⁻¹' {y})`. |
| 6 | Mathlib conventions | $d\mathcal L^m y$ is Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^m$; on `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin m)` the `volume` instance *is* Lebesgue measure (the pushforward of the product measure under the measurable equiv). Using `μH[(m:ℝ)]` on the target would differ by a dimensional constant — absorbed by `c`, but semantically farther from the text. | ✅ `∂volume` on `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin m)`. |
| 7 | Hypothesis completeness | Both halves of $m < s < n$ are needed, and no measurability of $A$ may be assumed (the text allows an arbitrary $A \subset \mathbb{R}^n$, and Hausdorff measures are outer measures on all sets). The exponent $s - m$ must be the real difference. | ✅ `(m : ℝ) < s ∧ s < n` as an implication hypothesis; no measurability on `A`; `μH[s - m]` with real subtraction. ⚠️ The two-sided bound would be more idiomatic as two separate hypotheses, and `(K : ℝ≥0∞) ^ (m : ℝ)` (rpow) could be the simpler `^ m` (npow). |
