**Statement:** [krylov_6_5_3_smooth_domain_dirichlet_solvability.md](krylov_6_5_3_smooth_domain_dirichlet_solvability.md) · **Lean:** [krylov_6_5_3_smooth_domain_dirichlet_solvability.lean](krylov_6_5_3_smooth_domain_dirichlet_solvability.lean)

The theorem is the classical Schauder solvability of the Dirichlet problem: for $f \in C^{k+\delta}(\Omega)$ and $g \in C^{k+2+\delta}(\bar\Omega)$ there is exactly one $u \in C^{k+2+\delta}(\Omega)$ with $Lu = f$ inside and $u = g$ on $\partial\Omega$. Because the statement is entirely carried by the standing assumptions, a faithful formalization has to supply them all: a bounded domain with smooth boundary, a second-order uniformly elliptic $L$ with $C^{k+\delta}$ coefficients, *and* the sign condition on the zeroth-order coefficient without which the Dirichlet problem is not uniquely solvable. On the encoding side the delicate point is what "$u = g$ on $\partial\Omega$" means: since a Lean function is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}^d$, its value at a boundary point is unrelated to its behaviour inside unless continuity up to $\bar\Omega$ is part of the solution concept — and both the existence and the uniqueness halves silently depend on that.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.
