## Notation

$Q$ is the **infinite** cylinder $(-\infty,T)\times\Omega$ with $T \in (-\infty,\infty]$: time extends to $-\infty$, so there is no initial surface and no initial condition, and $T = \infty$ is allowed. $\Omega$ is a bounded domain of class $C^{2+\delta}$ in the sense of Definition 6.1.6: near every boundary point the boundary can be straightened by a map whose $C^{2+\delta}$ norms, and those of its inverse, are bounded by one constant common to all boundary points. $\partial'Q$ is the parabolic boundary; for this cylinder it is the **lateral surface** $(-\infty,T)\times\partial\Omega$ and nothing else — there is no bottom cap to include, and the top ($\{T\}\times\Omega$, when $T$ is finite) is excluded as always for forward parabolic problems. Reading $\partial'Q$ with the finite-cylinder convention "bottom plus lateral side" is wrong here. $L$ acts in the space variables only, $Lu = a^{ij}D_{ij}u + b^iD_iu + cu$ with summation over repeated indices; $u_t$ enters as the separate term in $Lu - u_t = f$. The coefficients are the standing ones of Chapter 10: real, defined for all $(t,x)$, $a$ symmetric, uniformly nondegenerate with constant of ellipticity $\kappa > 0$, belonging to $C^{\delta/2,\,\delta}$ of the whole space, and $c \le 0$. The sign condition on $c$ is substance, not convention: on a cylinder without a bottom, uniqueness has no initial condition to lean on and rests on the sign of $c$; the exponential-in-time substitution that absorbs a positive $c$ on finite cylinders is useless when $t$ is unbounded below. The exponent pairs $(\delta/2,\delta)$ and $(1+\delta/2,2+\delta)$ are locked by the parabolic scaling — the time exponent is always half the space exponent. Membership in $C^{1+\delta/2,\,2+\delta}$ means $u$, $D_xu$, $D^2_xu$ and $u_t$ exist and are bounded, with the top-order data Hölder; the exponents never apply to $u$ itself. The solution is sought in $C^{1+\delta/2,\,2+\delta}(\bar Q)$, regular up to the closure, while $f$ and $g$ are taken in their classes on $Q$; uniqueness is among solutions of the same class.
