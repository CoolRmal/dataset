# Criteria: krylov_10_3_3_parabolic_dirichlet_domain_solvability

**Statement:** [krylov_10_3_3_parabolic_dirichlet_domain_solvability.md](krylov_10_3_3_parabolic_dirichlet_domain_solvability.md) · **Lean:** [krylov_10_3_3_parabolic_dirichlet_domain_solvability.lean](krylov_10_3_3_parabolic_dirichlet_domain_solvability.lean)

The Cauchy–Dirichlet problem $Lu - u_t = f$ in $Q$, $u = g$ on $\partial'Q$, is where every parabolic subtlety of the book comes together: the data are prescribed on the **parabolic** boundary — the initial surface and the lateral surface, *never* the terminal one, since prescribing the top would make the problem a backward one — the solution must attain those data as a limit from inside $Q$, the domain must be regular enough for a $C^{2+\delta}$-up-to-the-boundary solution to exist, and the anisotropic spaces must be the genuine parabolic ones. No sign condition on the zeroth-order coefficient is needed here (unlike the elliptic Theorem 6.5.3): the substitution $v = e^{-Kt}u$ absorbs a bounded $c$. So the audit reduces to three questions — is $\partial'Q$ the right set, is the boundary condition tied to the interior behaviour, and is $Q$ assumed regular?

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Solution concept | A classical solution must be continuous up to $\partial'Q$ and attain $g$ there as a limit. Since Lean functions are total, the value at a boundary point is otherwise unrelated to the interior. | ❗ **Genuine defect: the ground truth is false as stated.** `ParabolicDirichletSolution Q L f g u = (∀ p ∈ Q, L u p - deriv (fun t ↦ u (t, p.2)) p.1 = f p) ∧ ∀ p ∈ parabolicBoundary Q, u p = g p` has no continuity clause. Take $Q = (0,1)\times B$, $L = \Delta$, $f = g = 0$, $u \equiv 0$ and $v = \mathbf 1_Q$: on the open set $Q$, $v$ is locally constant, so `ParabolicHolderOn (2+δ) Q v` holds (all slice gauges equal $1$), $\Delta v - v_t = 0$ in $Q$, and $v = 0$ on `parabolicBoundary Q ⊆ frontier Q`. Both satisfy every clause, so the uniqueness conjunct `Set.EqOn v u (Q ∪ parabolicBoundary Q)` fails. `LaplaceDirichletSolution` in `krylov_2_3_1_green_poisson_representation` shows the missing clause: `ContinuousOn u (closure Q)`. |
| 2 | Domains / boundary | $\partial'Q$ must contain the initial surface and the lateral surface and must **exclude** the terminal surface: $p \in \partial Q$ belongs to $\partial'Q$ unless $Q$ contains a whole backward neighbourhood of $p$. | ❗ **The encoding is inverted.** `parabolicBoundary Q = {p ∈ frontier Q \| ∀ ε > 0, ∃ q ∈ Q, q.1 ≤ p.1 ∧ dist q p < ε}` selects the boundary points that *are* approachable from $Q$ at earlier times. For $Q = (0,1)\times B$: a top point $(1,x)$, $x \in B$, qualifies (take $q = (1-\varepsilon/2, x)$), while a bottom point $(0,x)$ does not (every $q \in Q$ has $q_1 > 0$). So the ground truth prescribes data on the terminal surface and none on the initial surface — a backward problem, for which existence in $C^{1+\delta/2,2+\delta}$ fails. The complement-style definition ("no backward neighbourhood inside $Q$") is the faithful one. |
| 3 | Domains / boundary | Existence of a solution with two bounded spatial derivatives up to $\partial'Q$ requires regularity of $\partial Q$ (Krylov's $Q$ is a cylinder over a smooth domain, or a domain of class $C^{2+\delta}$), plus the usual compatibility of $f$ and $g$ at the corner. | ❗ `hQ : IsOpen Q ∧ Bornology.IsBounded Q ∧ Q.Nonempty` assumes nothing about $\partial Q$: punctured, cusped or Lebesgue-spine domains are admitted, for which the Cauchy–Dirichlet problem has no solution attaining the data. Compare `SmoothBoundedDomain` in `krylov_6_5_3_smooth_domain_dirichlet_solvability`, which at least demands a smooth defining function; here even `RegularBoundedDomain` is not required. (This defect is currently masked by the missing continuity of row 1, which decouples the boundary condition entirely.) |
| 4 | Faithful encoding of the spaces | $C^{\delta/2,\delta}(Q)$ and $C^{1+\delta/2,2+\delta}(Q)$ are single-norm anisotropic spaces with constants uniform over $Q$. | ⚠️ `ParabolicHolderOn r Q u` requires `HolderOn r` on each time slice `{x \| (t,x) ∈ Q}` and `HolderOnReal (r/2)` on each spatial fibre `{t \| (t,x) ∈ Q}`, with **no uniformity across slices** and no mixed condition; and `HolderOnReal` contributes no sup-bound at all. So "$u \in C^{1+\delta/2,2+\delta}(Q)$" is strictly weaker than the text, which in this file weakens existence and strengthens uniqueness. The exponent bookkeeping itself is right: `ParabolicHolderOn (2+δ)` gives $C^{2+\delta}$ in $x$ and $C^{(2+\delta)/2} = C^{1+\delta/2}$ in $t$, and `ParabolicHolderOn δ` gives $C^{\delta/2,\delta}$. |
| 5 | Uniform parabolicity | $a^{ij}\xi_i\xi_j \ge \kappa\lvert\xi\rvert^2$ with $\kappa > 0$ uniform over $Q$, together with bounded coefficients; symmetry of $(a^{ij})$ is the usual normalization. | ⚠️ `ParabolicOperator L` supplies `0 < κ` and `∀ p ξ, κ * ‖ξ‖^2 ≤ ∑ i, ∑ j, a p i j * ξ i * ξ j` (with `‖ξ‖` the sup norm of `Fin d → ℝ`, equivalent up to a constant), and the ellipticity is required at *all* $p$, not only on $Q$ — both harmless. It imposes no symmetry of `a` (harmless: only the symmetric part is determined by `L`, and the quadratic form sees only that part) and **no bound** on `a`, `b`, `c`; boundedness is supposed to come from `hcoeff`, but the slice-wise gauges of row 4 do not give a uniform bound over $Q$. |
| 6 | Hypothesis structure | The parabolicity hypothesis and the coefficient-regularity hypothesis should concern the same coefficient triple. | ⚠️ `ParabolicOperator L` and `ParabolicOperatorCoefficientsHolder δ Q L` each existentially quantify their own `(a, b, c)`. Benign in effect — the `formula` clause holds for all input functions, so testing on polynomials recovers $c$, then $b$, then the symmetric part of $a$ from `L` — but a single bundled triple would avoid relying on that argument. |
| 7 | Hypothesis completeness | Whether a sign condition on $c$ is needed is a genuine mathematical question, and the answer differs from the elliptic case. | ✅ Correctly absent. For the parabolic Cauchy–Dirichlet problem a bounded $c$ of arbitrary sign is fine ($v = e^{-Kt}u$ reduces to $c - K \le 0$), so the omission that makes `krylov_6_5_3_smooth_domain_dirichlet_solvability` false is not a defect here. |
| 8 | Conclusion completeness / faithful encoding | Existence and uniqueness, with uniqueness stated on the set where the problem lives, and the equation $Lu - u_t = f$ with $\partial_t$ taken along the time fibre. | ✅ `∃ u, ParabolicHolderOn (2+δ) Q u ∧ ParabolicDirichletSolution Q L f g u ∧ ∀ v, … → Set.EqOn v u (Q ∪ parabolicBoundary Q)` — the right shape (a bare `∃!` would compare values outside $Q$), and `L u p - deriv (fun t ↦ u (t, p.2)) p.1 = f p` is the classical equation, with `deriv` legitimate because `ParabolicHolderOn` supplies `ContDiffOn ℝ 1` along each fibre. ❗ Predicted error: dropping the uniqueness clause, or asserting `Set.EqOn` on `closure Q` (which would include the terminal surface, where nothing is prescribed). |
