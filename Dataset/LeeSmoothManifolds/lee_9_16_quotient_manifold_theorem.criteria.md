# Criteria: lee_9_16_quotient_manifold_theorem

**Statement:** [lee_9_16_quotient_manifold_theorem.md](lee_9_16_quotient_manifold_theorem.md) · **Lean:** [lee_9_16_quotient_manifold_theorem.lean](lee_9_16_quotient_manifold_theorem.lean)

This is the hardest of the ten to state: Mathlib has no quotient-manifold construction, so the conclusion must existentially produce a *type* `Q` together with a topology, a `ChartedSpace` structure over $\mathbb{R}^{m-g}$, an `IsManifold` instance, and a projection `π` whose fibers are exactly the $G$-orbits — and then the **uniqueness** of that smooth structure. All four adjectives of the hypothesis (Lie group, smooth, free, proper) carry weight: dropping properness makes the theorem false (the irrational-slope line acting on $T^2$ is a smooth free action whose orbit space is not a manifold). The other pressure points are the truncated subtraction `m - g` and the fact that Lee's "topological manifold" bakes in Hausdorffness of $M/G$, which is precisely what properness buys and which Mathlib's `ChartedSpace`/`IsManifold` do **not** provide.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Hypothesis completeness | All five ingredients of "a Lie group acts smoothly, freely and properly" must be present. `SmoothFreeProperAction act` bundles: the action axioms `act 1 x = x` and `act (a * b) x = act a (act b x)`; joint smoothness `ContMDiff (𝓘(ℝ, Fin g → ℝ).prod 𝓘(ℝ, Fin m → ℝ)) 𝓘(ℝ, Fin m → ℝ) ∞ (fun p : G × M ↦ act p.1 p.2)`; freeness `∀ a x, act a x = x → a = 1`; properness. | ✅ All five. ❗ Predicted errors: (a) dropping properness — the theorem is then false; (b) stating smoothness of `act a : M → M` for each fixed `a` instead of joint smoothness on `G × M`, which is strictly weaker; (c) omitting the action axioms, leaving `act` an arbitrary function. |
| 2 | Faithful encoding | "Proper action" means the map $G \times M \to M \times M$, $(a,x) \mapsto (a\cdot x, x)$, is proper. | ✅ `IsProperMap fun p : G × M ↦ (act p.1 p.2, p.2)` is literally that. ⚠️ Candidates using the equivalent-for-locally-compact-Hausdorff formulation "`{a \| (a • K) ∩ K ≠ ∅}` is compact for every compact `K`", or Mathlib's `[ProperSMul G M]` class, should be judged acceptable — but note that equivalence is nontrivial and Mathlib-unproven in this generality. Properness of `act` itself (rather than of the twisted map) is **not** equivalent and is a real error. |
| 3 | Conclusion completeness | Hausdorffness of the quotient. Lee asserts $M/G$ is a *topological manifold*, which in his convention means locally Euclidean **plus Hausdorff plus second countable**, and separating orbits is exactly what properness is for — it is the substantive half of the theorem. The Lean conclusion produces only `TopologicalSpace Q`, `ChartedSpace (Fin (m - g) → ℝ) Q` and `IsManifold 𝓘(ℝ, Fin (m - g) → ℝ) ∞ Q`. | ⚠️ Real loss of content: `T2Space Q` (and `SecondCountableTopology Q`) should be among the existentially produced instances. Correspondingly, `[T2Space M]` and `[SecondCountableTopology M]` are missing from the hypotheses, so as stated the theorem cannot even be asked to deliver them. |
| 4 | Conclusion completeness | "$\dim M/G = \dim M - \dim G$" is `ChartedSpace (Fin (m - g) → ℝ) Q`, where `m - g` is **truncated** ℕ-subtraction. | ⚠️ No falsehood is introduced (a free proper action of a $g$-dimensional Lie group on a nonempty $m$-manifold forces $g \le m$, and for `M = ∅` any dimension works), but truncation is a classic trap and a candidate that guards with `hgm : g ≤ m`, or that phrases the dimension via `Fin m → ℝ` and a complement, is being more careful. |
| 5 | Conclusion completeness | The projection must (i) exist, (ii) be surjective, (iii) have exactly the orbits as fibers, (iv) be a smooth submersion. | ✅ `Surjective π`, `∀ x y, π x = π y ↔ ∃ a, act a x = y`, `Manifold.IsSubmersion 𝓘(ℝ, Fin m → ℝ) 𝓘(ℝ, Fin (m - g) → ℝ) ∞ π`. Note that no separate "`Q` carries the quotient topology" clause is needed: a surjective submersion is continuous and open, hence a quotient map, so the topology on `Q` is pinned down. The `↔` (not just `→`) is essential — `→` alone would allow `Q` to be a point. |
| 6 | Conclusion completeness | "has a **unique** smooth structure with the property that $\pi$ is a smooth submersion". | ✅ Rendered as: for every `Q'` with topology, charted-space and `IsManifold` structure and every `π'` that is surjective, orbit-separating and a smooth submersion, there is a `Diffeomorph 𝓘(ℝ, Fin (m - g) → ℝ) 𝓘(ℝ, Fin (m - g) → ℝ) Q Q' ∞` with `e ∘ π = π'`. That is the correct categorical rendering, and `e` is automatically unique because `π` is surjective. ❗ Predicted error: omitting the uniqueness clause altogether (it is the second half of the sentence), or weakening `Diffeomorph` to `Homeomorph`/`Equiv`. |
| 7 | Mathlib conventions | `[LieGroup 𝓘(ℝ, Fin g → ℝ) ∞ G]` does give `G` a smooth-manifold structure: `LieGroup` extends `ContMDiffMul`, which extends `IsManifold`, and adds smooth inversion. So `G` is genuinely a Lie group and no separate `[IsManifold … G]` is needed. | ✅ for `G`. ⚠️ for the action: Mathlib has `[MulAction G M]`, `[ContMDiffSMul I n G M]` and `[ProperSMul G M]`; a formulation `ContMDiff … (fun p : G × M ↦ p.1 • p.2)` plus those classes would be more idiomatic and would inherit the `MulAction` API. Hand-rolling is defensible because the theorem quantifies over a bare `act : G → M → M`, but the group-action axioms then have to be (and are) restated by hand. |
| 8 | Semantic closeness | `Q : Type v` lives in the same universe as `M`, which is correct — the orbit space is a quotient of `M`. Binding the instances as `∃ (_ : TopologicalSpace Q) …` makes them inaccessible but still available to instance search inside the body. | ✅ ⚠️ A `Σ`-style bundling or a `∃ Q : Type v, ∃ _ : …` chain is the only option without a construction in Mathlib; the resulting statement is heavy but faithful. |
