# Criteria: grafakos_5_6_6_vector_valued_maximal

**Statement:** [grafakos_5_6_6_vector_valued_maximal.md](grafakos_5_6_6_vector_valued_maximal.md) · **Lean:** [grafakos_5_6_6_vector_valued_maximal.lean](grafakos_5_6_6_vector_valued_maximal.lean)

The Fefferman–Stein inequalities bound the $\ell^r$-norm of the sequence $(Mf_j)_j$ — the $\ell^r$ *inside* the $L^p$, which is what makes the result non-trivial; the interchanged form $\big(\sum_j\|Mf_j\|_p^r\big)^{1/r}\lesssim\big(\sum_j\|f_j\|_p^r\big)^{1/r}$ is an immediate consequence of the scalar maximal theorem and is *not* this theorem. Two claims must both appear (the weak $(1,1)$ bound and the strong $(p,p)$ bound), and the interesting quantitative content is the constant's structure: $C_n$ depends only on the dimension, the $r$-dependence of the weak bound is exactly $1+(r-1)^{-1}$, and it blows up as $r\downarrow1$. Everything is `ℝ≥0∞`-valued, which conveniently removes all summability and integrability side conditions.
