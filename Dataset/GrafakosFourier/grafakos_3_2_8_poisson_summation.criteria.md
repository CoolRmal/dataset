# Criteria: grafakos_3_2_8_poisson_summation

**Statement:** [grafakos_3_2_8_poisson_summation.md](grafakos_3_2_8_poisson_summation.md) · **Lean:** [grafakos_3_2_8_poisson_summation.lean](grafakos_3_2_8_poisson_summation.lean)

Poisson summation is an identity between two infinite sums, so the formalization stands or falls on the hypotheses that make both sides absolutely convergent: the pointwise decay $|f(x)| \le C(1+|x|)^{-n-\delta}$ controls the periodization $\sum_k f(x+k)$, and the separate hypothesis $\sum_m |\widehat f(m)| < \infty$ controls the Fourier side (it does *not* follow from the decay). If either is dropped, `tsum` silently returns `0` on the divergent side and the "theorem" becomes a false or vacuous statement about junk. A second, quieter issue is that the constant-free form of the identity — lattice $\mathbb{Z}^n$, character $e^{2\pi i m\cdot x}$, no prefactor — is tied to Grafakos's $e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}$ normalization of $\widehat{f}$.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Hypothesis completeness (convergence of the spatial side) | The decay $\lvert f(x)\rvert \le C(1+\lvert x\rvert)^{-n-\delta}$, $C,\delta>0$, is what makes $\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^n} f(x+k)$ absolutely convergent (and its periodization continuous). Without it the right-hand `tsum` is the junk value `0` and the identity is false. | ✅ `∃ C δ : ℝ, 0 < C ∧ 0 < δ ∧ ∀ x, ‖f x‖ ≤ C * (1 + ‖x‖) ^ (-(n : ℝ) - δ)` — the existential over `C, δ` is inside the hypothesis, exactly "for some $C,\delta>0$", and the exponent is a `Real.rpow` on the positive base `1 + ‖x‖`. ❗ Trap: weakening to $O(\lvert x\rvert^{-n})$ (borderline, insufficient) or to mere integrability. |
| 2 | Hypothesis completeness (convergence of the Fourier side) | $\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^n}\lvert\widehat f(m)\rvert < \infty$ is an *independent* hypothesis — the decay of $f$ gives no decay of $\widehat f$. In a Banach space over a countable index, `Summable` is unconditional summability, equivalent to absolute summability for `ℂ`. | ✅ `Summable fun m : Fin n → ℤ ↦ 𝓕 f ((WithLp.toLp 2 fun i ↦ (m i : ℝ)))`. ❗ Trap: omitting it (then the left `tsum` is junk `0`), or replacing it with a decay hypothesis on `𝓕 f` that the text does not assume. |
| 3 | Junk values (`𝓕 f` itself) | `𝓕 f w = ∫ v, 𝐞 (-⟪v,w⟫) • f v` is `0` unless `f` is integrable, so the Fourier coefficients $\widehat f(m)$ are meaningless without integrability. Continuity plus the decay hypothesis already imply it, but stating it makes the encoding self-evidently junk-free. | ✅ `Integrable f` is listed as a hypothesis. ⚠️ Formally redundant (it follows from `Continuous f` and the decay bound), so a candidate omitting it is still faithful — but a candidate omitting *both* it and the decay bound has a statement about the constant function `0`. |
| 4 | Faithful encoding (lattice and character) | $\mathbb{Z}^n \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ must be embedded as the integer lattice of the *Euclidean* space, and the character must be $e^{2\pi i m\cdot x}$ with the sign opposite to the transform's $e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}$. | ✅ `WithLp.toLp 2 fun i ↦ (m i : ℝ)` for the embedding `(Fin n → ℤ) → EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n)`, and `Complex.exp (2 * Real.pi * Complex.I * (∑ i, (m i : ℂ) * (x i : ℂ)))` for $e^{2\pi i m\cdot x}$ — the dual pairing matches `innerₗ` used by `𝓕`, and the signs are consistent. ❗ Trap: the character $e^{im\cdot x}$ together with the $2\pi$-convention transform, which makes the identity false; or summing over `ℤ^n` on one side and `ℕ^n` on the other. |
| 5 | Conclusion completeness | Both conclusions are asserted: the identity for all $x$, and the "in particular" specialization at $x=0$. | ✅ A conjunction of `∀ x : EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n), ∑' m, … = ∑' k, f (x + …)` with the $x = 0$ instance `(∑' m, 𝓕 f …) = ∑' k, f (…)`. ⚠️ The second conjunct is a literal consequence of the first; keeping it matches the text's "and in particular" and is harmless. |
| 6 | Semantic closeness (summability as part of the claim) | The textbook identity presupposes both sides converge; a maximally informative formalization would *assert* `Summable (fun k ↦ f (x + k))` alongside the equality rather than leaving it implicit in a `tsum`. | ⚠️ The ground truth states only the equality of `tsum`s. Since summability of the right-hand side is forced by the decay hypothesis this is sound, but a candidate that additionally concludes `Summable (fun k : Fin n → ℤ ↦ f (x + …))` is strictly more informative and should be rewarded. |
| 7 | Mathlib conventions | `𝓕` (the `FourierTransform` instance on `V → E` for a finite-dimensional real inner-product space), `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n)` with `volume` = Lebesgue, `tsum`/`Summable` over the countable index `Fin n → ℤ`, `Real.rpow` for the non-integer decay exponent. | ✅ All idiomatic; the index type `Fin n → ℤ` is the natural `ℤ^n`. ⚠️ The hypotheses are bundled as one anonymous four-fold conjunction `hf : Continuous f ∧ Integrable f ∧ … ∧ Summable …`; separate named hypotheses would be more usable and more legible. |
