# Criteria: grafakos_1_3_4_riesz_thorin_interpolation

**Statement:** [grafakos_1_3_4_riesz_thorin_interpolation.md](grafakos_1_3_4_riesz_thorin_interpolation.md) · **Lean:** [grafakos_1_3_4_riesz_thorin_interpolation.lean](grafakos_1_3_4_riesz_thorin_interpolation.lean)

The content is the multiplicative interpolation of *two* pairs of exponents — $(p_0,q_0)$ and $(p_1,q_1)$ — with the geometric-mean constant $M_0^{1-\theta}M_1^{\theta}$, for a **complex**-linear operator. Two things decide the quality of the encoding: the exponent arithmetic $1/p = (1-\theta)/p_0 + \theta/p_1$, which must survive $p_0 = \infty$ or $q_1 = \infty$ (the whole reason one interpolates in the first place) and therefore has to live in `ℝ≥0∞` where `(∞)⁻¹ = 0`, rather than as a real division; and the treatment of the operator's domain, since Grafakos states the hypotheses on *finitely simple functions* and only then extends by density.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Junk values (exponent arithmetic) | $1/p = (1-\theta)/p_0 + \theta/p_1$ must be stated so that $p_j = \infty$ is legal and $p$ is pinned down. In `ℝ≥0∞`, `(⊤)⁻¹ = 0` and `(0)⁻¹ = ⊤`, and `ENNReal.inv_inv` makes `p⁻¹ = …` determine `p`; a real-valued `1/p = (1-θ)/p₀ + θ/p₁` cannot express $p_0 = \infty$ at all and evaluates `1/0 = 0` as junk. | ✅ `hp : p⁻¹ = ENNReal.ofReal (1 - θ) * p₀⁻¹ + ENNReal.ofReal θ * p₁⁻¹` and the same for `q`, with `p₀ p₁ q₀ q₁ p q : ℝ≥0∞`. This is the junk-free formulation and admits every endpoint case. ❗ Trap: real exponents with `1/p₀`, or an `ENNReal` version writing `(1 - θ)` as `1 - ENNReal.ofReal θ` (truncated subtraction is fine here but `ENNReal.ofReal (1 - θ)` is the safer literal reading). |
| 2 | Faithful encoding (linearity) | Riesz–Thorin is a *complex* interpolation theorem: the three-lines proof needs $\mathbb{C}$-linearity, and the real-scalar analogue fails with these constants. | ✅ `T : (X → ℂ) →ₗ[ℂ] (Y → ℂ)`. ❗ Trap: `→ₗ[ℝ]`, or a bare `T : (X → ℂ) → Y → ℂ` with a hand-written additivity hypothesis (which would then also have to be homogeneous over `ℂ`, not just `ℝ`). |
| 3 | Semantic closeness (domain) & conclusion completeness | The text's hypotheses and first conclusion are about *finitely simple* functions, with "consequently, when $p < \infty$, by density, $T$ has a unique bounded extension from $L^p$ to $L^q$" as a second conclusion. The ground truth instead assumes `HasStrongType` on all of $L^{p_0}$, $L^{p_1}$ and concludes on all of $L^p$. | ⚠️ A defensible reformulation (the extension clause becomes vacuous once $T$ is given on the whole $L^{p_j}$), but the "unique bounded extension" statement — the part that requires $p < \infty$ and density of simple functions — is silently dropped, and the simple-function hypothesis is strengthened to all of $L^{p_j}$. A formalization keeping `SimpleFunc`-valued hypotheses plus a `∃! extension` clause would be strictly more faithful. |
| 4 | Hypothesis completeness | Grafakos assumes **both** $(X,\mu)$ and $(Y,\nu)$ $\sigma$-finite. | ⚠️ Neither `[SigmaFinite μ]` nor `[SigmaFinite ν]` appears, making the Lean statement formally stronger than the text (and stronger than the sibling `grafakos_1_3_2`, which does carry `[SigmaFinite μ]`). Adding both typeclasses would match the source; a candidate that includes them must not be marked down. |
| 5 | Conclusion completeness (constant) | The interpolated bound is the *explicit* geometric mean $M_0^{1-\theta}M_1^{\theta}$, not merely "some constant". | ✅ `HasStrongType μ ν T p q (ENNReal.rpow M₀ (1 - θ) * ENNReal.rpow M₁ θ)`; `ENNReal.rpow` is junk-free for `0 ≤ M < ∞` and `0 < θ < 1`. ❗ Trap: `∃ C, …`, or `M₀ ^ θ * M₁ ^ (1 - θ)` with the two exponents swapped relative to the two endpoint hypotheses. |
| 6 | Hypothesis completeness | All of: $0 < \theta < 1$ (strictly — the endpoints are the hypotheses themselves), $1 \le p_0,p_1,q_0,q_1$, and finiteness of the two operator norms. | ✅ `hθ : 0 < θ ∧ θ < 1`, `hexponents : 1 ≤ p₀ ∧ 1 ≤ p₁ ∧ 1 ≤ q₀ ∧ 1 ≤ q₁` (upper bounds by `∞` are automatic in `ℝ≥0∞`), `hM₀ : M₀ < ∞`, `hM₁ : M₁ < ∞`. ❗ Trap: `0 ≤ θ ≤ 1`, or omitting `1 ≤ p₀` etc., which the complex method genuinely needs. |
| 7 | Junk values (measurability) | `HasStrongType` demands `MemLp (T f) q ν`, i.e. `AEStronglyMeasurable (T f) ν`, which the text supplies by fiat ("taking values in the set of measurable functions"). Here it is *derivable*: for $p_0 \le p \le p_1$, split $f = f\mathbf{1}_{\lvert f\rvert>1} + f\mathbf{1}_{\lvert f\rvert\le1} \in L^{p_0}+L^{p_1}$ and use linearity of `T` plus `h₀`, `h₁`. | ✅ Sound — unlike the sublinear Marcinkiewicz statement, no measurability hypothesis is needed because `T` is a `→ₗ[ℂ]`. |
| 8 | Semantic closeness (quantifier placement) | $M_0$, $M_1$, $\theta$ and all six exponents are *given* data, and the conclusion's constant is a formula in them — not an existentially quantified constant, and not a constant allowed to depend on $f$. | ✅ Implicit binders `{p₀ p₁ q₀ q₁ p q : ℝ≥0∞} {θ : ℝ} {M₀ M₁ : ℝ≥0∞}` precede the `∀ f` hidden inside `HasStrongType`, so the constant is uniform in `f`. |
