# Criteria: kong_5_4_2_hopf_friedrich_dichotomy

**Statement:** [kong_5_4_2_hopf_friedrich_dichotomy.md](kong_5_4_2_hopf_friedrich_dichotomy.md) · **Lean:** [kong_5_4_2_hopf_friedrich_dichotomy.lean](kong_5_4_2_hopf_friedrich_dichotomy.lean)

A faithful formalization must encode the standing Hopf hypotheses (the origin is an equilibrium for every parameter value; the linearization at $\mu = 0$ has eigenvalues $\pm i\beta$ with $\beta > 0$; the degeneracy $\alpha'(0) = 0$) and then state a three-way disjunction: the center alternative (all nearby orbits at $\mu = 0$ closed, no nearby closed orbits for $\mu \ne 0$), or the birth of a unique limit cycle for small $\mu > 0$ *only*, or for small $\mu < 0$ *only*, with the cycle shrinking to the origin and its period tending to $2\pi/\beta$. Encoding the spectral hypotheses through the trace and determinant of the linearization is the junk-free move; the residual weaknesses are all in the conclusion — the exclusivity of "only", what "$\Gamma(\mu) \to (0,0)$" measures, which side the limits are taken from, and whether $T(\mu)$ is the *least* period.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Faithful encoding | The eigenvalue hypotheses $\alpha(0) = 0$, $\beta(0) = \beta > 0$, $\alpha'(0) = 0$ must be expressed without introducing $\alpha, \beta$ as unconstrained existential functions. For a $2\times 2$ real matrix with eigenvalues $\alpha \pm i\beta$ one has $\operatorname{tr} = 2\alpha$, $\det = \alpha^2 + \beta^2$. | ✅ `Matrix.trace (linearizationMatrix F 0) = 0`, `Matrix.det (linearizationMatrix F 0) = β ^ 2` with `hβ : 0 < β`, and `HasDerivAt (fun μ ↦ Matrix.trace (linearizationMatrix F μ)) 0 0` for $\alpha'(0) = 0$; together with `∀ μ, F 0 μ = 0`. ❗ Trap: `∃ α β : ℝ → ℝ, spectrum … = {α μ + β μ * I, …}` with no regularity, where `α'(0) = 0` then says nothing. |
| 2 | Mathlib conventions | In current mathlib `ContDiff 𝕜 n` takes `n : WithTop ℕ∞`, where `⊤` is `ω` (**analytic**) and `∞` is $C^\infty$. The Hopf–Friedrich dichotomy is an analytic theorem — for merely $C^\infty$ families the center/focus alternative can fail — so `⊤` is defensible here. | ✅/❗ `hF.1 : ContDiff ℝ ⊤ (fun p : (Fin 2 → ℝ) × ℝ ↦ F p.1 p.2)` therefore assumes analyticity in $(x,\mu)$, matching the analytic hypothesis (5.4.2). Trap in both directions: a candidate writing `⊤` while *meaning* $C^\infty$ has a silent convention error, and one writing `ContDiff ℝ ∞` weakens the hypothesis to $C^\infty$, where the dichotomy is not available. |
| 3 | Faithful encoding / junk values | The linearization must be the Jacobian in $x$ at the origin, for the given $\mu$. | ✅ `linearizationMatrix F μ i j := fderiv ℝ (fun x ↦ F x μ) 0 (Pi.single j 1) i` — the columns are the directional derivatives along the standard basis, evaluated at `0`. The `fderiv` is junk-free because smoothness is assumed in the same hypothesis block. ❗ Trap: differentiating with respect to the wrong variable, or at the wrong base point. |
| 4 | Conclusion completeness | The center alternative has two conjuncts: all nearby orbits of (5.4.1-0) are closed, **and** (5.4.1-$\mu$) has no nearby closed orbits for small $\mu \ne 0$. Dropping the second conjunct makes the alternative far too easy. | ✅ Both present, the first restricted to nonconstant trajectories with `‖x 0‖ < ε`, the second as `∀ μ, 0 < \|μ\| → \|μ\| < ε → ¬∃ x, IsClosedOrbit (fun y ↦ F y μ) x ∧ ‖x 0‖ < ε`. ⚠️ "orbits in a neighborhood of $(0,0)$" is encoded by a bound on the *initial point* `‖x 0‖ < ε` only, not by the whole orbit staying in the neighborhood. |
| 5 | Conclusion completeness | Kong's "for $\mu > 0$ sufficiently close to zero **only** or for $\mu < 0$ sufficiently close to zero **only**" asserts exclusivity: cycles appear on one side and not on the other. | ⚠️ `hopf true` says nothing about $\mu < 0$ (and vice versa), so the exclusivity is silently dropped; the disjunction `center ∨ hopf true ∨ hopf false` is therefore weaker than the text. A faithful version conjoins "and no closed orbits near the origin for $\mu$ on the other side". |
| 6 | Semantic closeness | "$\Gamma(\mu) \to (0,0)$ as $\mu \to 0$" is convergence of the whole cycle to the origin, and the limit is one-sided (only one side of $\mu = 0$ carries a cycle). | ⚠️ `Tendsto (fun μ ↦ ‖orbit μ 0‖) (𝓝[≠] 0) (𝓝 0)` measures only the base point `orbit μ 0`; `⨆ t, ‖orbit μ t‖ → 0` would express $\Gamma(\mu) \to (0,0)$. ⚠️ The filter `𝓝[≠] 0` is two-sided, but `orbit`/`period` are constrained only on the relevant side, so their values on the other side are free junk the existential can pick; `𝓝[>] 0` / `𝓝[<] 0` would be exact. |
| 7 | Conclusion completeness | "Unique limit cycle" must be uniqueness *as an orbit*: any nearby closed orbit has the same image. | ✅ `∀ y, IsClosedOrbit (fun z ↦ F z μ) y → ‖y 0‖ < ε → range y = range (orbit μ)` — uniqueness of the set, not of the parametrization, which is the correct notion. |
| 8 | Semantic closeness | "$T(\mu) \to 2\pi/\beta$" is a statement about the *least* period of $\Gamma(\mu)$; any integer multiple of a period is also a period. | ⚠️ `0 < period μ ∧ ∀ t, orbit μ (t + period μ) = orbit μ t` asserts only that `period μ` *is* a period, so the limit `Tendsto period (𝓝[≠] 0) (𝓝 (2 * Real.pi / β))` constrains a chosen period rather than the minimal one. Adding minimality (e.g. `IsLeast {T \| 0 < T ∧ Function.Periodic (orbit μ) T} (period μ)`) would be faithful. |
