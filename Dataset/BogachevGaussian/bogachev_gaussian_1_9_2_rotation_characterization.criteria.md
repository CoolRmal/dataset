# Criteria: bogachev_gaussian_1_9_2_rotation_characterization

**Statement:** [bogachev_gaussian_1_9_2_rotation_characterization.md](bogachev_gaussian_1_9_2_rotation_characterization.md) · **Lean:** [bogachev_gaussian_1_9_2_rotation_characterization.lean](bogachev_gaussian_1_9_2_rotation_characterization.lean)

A faithful formalization must be an **equivalence**, must quantify over *every* angle $\varphi$, and must read "independent copies" as the joint law being the product $\mu\otimes\mu$ — not merely as equality of the two marginals. The rotation is the specific pair $(\xi_1\sin\varphi+\xi_2\cos\varphi,\ \xi_1\cos\varphi-\xi_2\sin\varphi)$; using a general orthogonal matrix gives a true but different (and much easier) statement, and the point of Kac's characterization is that this one-parameter family already suffices. The `centered` half of the conclusion must not be dropped: rotation invariance forces the mean to be $0$.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| 1 | Conclusion completeness | The statement is an `↔`. Only the direction "Gaussian ⟹ rotation invariance" is elementary; the converse is the theorem. | ✅ `(IsGaussian μ ∧ ∫ x, x ∂μ = 0) ↔ ∀ φ, …`. ❗ Predicted error: formalizing only the forward implication. |
| 2 | Faithful encoding | "$\xi_1,\xi_2$ independent copies" is the product measure `μ.prod μ`, and "the rotated pair are independent copies" is `(μ.prod μ).map T = μ.prod μ` — an equality of measures on $\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^n$. | ✅ Exactly this. ❗ Predicted error: asserting only that each rotated coordinate has law `μ` (equality of marginals), which is strictly weaker and false as a characterization. |
| 3 | Semantic closeness | The pair is $(\sin\varphi\,\xi_1+\cos\varphi\,\xi_2,\ \cos\varphi\,\xi_1-\sin\varphi\,\xi_2)$; the determinant is $-1$, so this is a reflection rather than a rotation, and swapping a sign changes the map. | ✅ Transcribed literally as `(sin φ • p.1 + cos φ • p.2, cos φ • p.1 - sin φ • p.2)`. ⚠️ The book notes it suffices to have the condition for a single $\varphi \ne k\pi/2$; the `∀ φ` form is the literal statement. |
| 4 | Hypothesis completeness | `μ` must be a probability measure; without it both sides are meaningless. | 6 | Junk values | `∫ x, x ∂μ` is `0` for a non-integrable identity; on a probability measure whose rotations are invariant this is not a loophole in the `←` direction only because integrability is part of the conclusion `IsGaussian`. | ⚠️ In the `→` direction the hypothesis `IsGaussian μ` supplies integrability (mathlib's `IsGaussian.integrable_id`), so the centering condition is genuine. |
