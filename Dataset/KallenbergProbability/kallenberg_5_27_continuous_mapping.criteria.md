# Criteria: kallenberg_5_27_continuous_mapping

**Statement:** [kallenberg_5_27_continuous_mapping.md](kallenberg_5_27_continuous_mapping.md) · **Lean:** [kallenberg_5_27_continuous_mapping.lean](kallenberg_5_27_continuous_mapping.lean)

Kallenberg's version of the continuous-mapping theorem is the *array* version: a whole sequence $f_1, f_2, \dots$ of merely measurable maps, tied to a limit map $f$ by the joint condition $s_n \to s \in C \Rightarrow f_n(s_n) \to f(s)$ on a set $C$ that is not assumed measurable, plus the requirement that $\xi$ lands in $C$ almost surely. The delicate encoding point is exactly this last clause: with $C \subset S$ arbitrary, "$\xi \in C$ a.s." has to be read through mathlib's `Measure` as an outer measure, `μ' (ξ ⁻¹' C) = 1`, which is *weaker* than `∀ᵐ ω ∂μ', ξ ω ∈ C` and therefore yields a stronger (still true) theorem — a candidate that instead assumes `MeasurableSet C` has weakened the result. The second requirement is that the "in particular" corollary about an a.s.-continuous $f$ is present, with a.s. continuity read as the law of $\xi$ charging the continuity-point set fully, not as continuity of $f$.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| 1 | Hypothesis completeness | The linking condition is about the whole array: `∀ s ∈ C, ∀ sn : ℕ → S, Tendsto sn atTop (𝓝 s) → Tendsto (fun n ↦ fn n (sn n)) atTop (𝓝 (f s))`. | 2 | Faithful encoding | `C : Set S` is not assumed measurable, so "$\xi \in C$ a.s." must be `μ' (ξ ⁻¹' C) = 1` with `μ'` acting as an outer measure. | 3 | Convergence mode | Both hypothesis and conclusion are convergence in distribution — `TendstoInDistribution … (fun _ ↦ μ) μ'`, weak convergence of the pushed-forward laws. | ✅ `hξ : TendstoInDistribution ξn atTop ξ (fun _ ↦ μ) μ'` and the conclusion `TendstoInDistribution (fun n ω ↦ fn n (ξn n ω)) atTop (fun ω ↦ f (ξ ω)) (fun _ ↦ μ) μ'`. ❗ Trap: a.s. or in-probability convergence, which would make the theorem false in the form stated (the $\xi_n$ and $\xi$ live on different spaces). |
| 4 | Hypothesis completeness | Measurability of the maps: the text says "measurable functions $f, f_1, f_2, \dots$", and no continuity is assumed anywhere. | ✅ `hfn : ∀ n, Measurable (fn n)`, `hf : Measurable f`. ❗ Trap: strengthening to `Continuous f` / `Continuous (fn n)` — that is the naive continuous-mapping theorem, not Kallenberg 5.27. |

| 6 | Faithful encoding | "$f$ is a.s. continuous at $\xi$" is a statement about the *set of continuity points* of the map, pulled back through $\xi$: `{s : S \| ContinuousAt g s}`, not `ContinuousAt g (ξ ω)` for a.e. `ω` phrased over a non-measurable predicate, and not `ContinuousOn g C`. | ✅ The continuity-point set is used directly, and it is a $G_\delta$ (hence measurable) in a metric space, so the outer-measure reading is unambiguous. |
| 7 | Semantic closeness | The corollary is stated as a second conjunct *under* the ambient hypotheses `hcontinuous`, `hC`, `hf`, `hfn` about the fixed `C`, `f`, `fn`. | ⚠️ Logically it still delivers the standalone corollary (instantiate the theorem at `C = univ`, `f = fn n = id`, for which `hcontinuous` and `hC` hold trivially), but a separate top-level theorem would be more idiomatic and would not require the reader to perform that instantiation. |
