# Criteria: engelking_5_2_8_normal_product_interval

**Statement:** [engelking_5_2_8_normal_product_interval.md](engelking_5_2_8_normal_product_interval.md) · **Lean:** [engelking_5_2_8_normal_product_interval.lean](engelking_5_2_8_normal_product_interval.lean)

This is Dowker's theorem, and its whole content is the equivalence of a conjunction on $X$ (normal **and** countably paracompact) with a single property of the product $X \times I$; both directions must be present and the two properties on the left must be stated as *conclusions* of the right-hand side, not as standing hypotheses. The formalization stands or falls on Engelking's separation conventions: his "normal" includes $T_1$ and his "countably paracompact" includes Hausdorff, whereas mathlib's `NormalSpace` includes nothing. Here the `[T1Space X]` instance is what keeps the statement true, and the countably-paracompact definition must restrict to *countable* open covers and demand a locally finite **open** refinement.

Legend: ✅ ground truth satisfies the criterion · ⚠️ ground truth acceptable but improvable · ❗ trap — known/likely model error to check in candidate statements.

| # | Category | Criterion / potential error | Assessment of ground truth |
|---|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Separation axioms (the Engelking convention) | Mathlib's `NormalSpace` does **not** imply $T_1$, while Engelking's "normal" does; the missing axiom must be supplied. Its omission genuinely breaks the `←` direction: for the two-point indiscrete space $X$, every closed set is $\emptyset$ or $X$, and $X \times I$ is normal (its closed sets are exactly $X \times C$ with $C$ closed in $I$), so `NormalSpace (X × I)` holds while $X$ is not Hausdorff and hence not countably paracompact. | ✅ `[T1Space X]` is assumed, so `NormalSpace X` together with it is exactly Engelking's "normal". ❗ Predicted error: dropping `[T1Space X]` (or `[T2Space X]`) and stating the bare mathlib iff. |
| 2 | Faithful encoding (auxiliary definition) | `IsCountablyParacompact` must carry Engelking's Hausdorff conjunct and quantify over **countable** open covers only, producing a refinement that is simultaneously an *open cover*, a *refinement*, and *locally finite*. Dropping openness of the refinement, or dropping the requirement that it still covers $X$, gives a strictly weaker notion. | ✅ `T2Space X ∧ ∀ (ι) (_ : Countable ι) (U : ι → Set X), IsOpenCover U → ∃ κ V, IsOpenCover V ∧ Refines V U ∧ LocallyFinite V`. |
| 3 | Semantic closeness (refinement direction) | `Refines V U := ∀ j, ∃ i, V j ⊆ U i`: every member of the refinement lies inside *some* member of the original cover. The reversed reading (`∀ i, ∃ j, V j ⊆ U i`) is a different and much weaker condition and is the classic quantifier error in covering statements. | ✅ Direction correct in `Defs.Refines`, used as `Refines V U` with `V` the new cover. ❗ Predicted error: reversed quantifiers, or `∀ j i, V j ⊆ U i`. |
| 4 | Conclusion completeness | The statement is an `↔`; the hard direction (normal + countably paracompact $\Rightarrow$ $X \times I$ normal) and the easy-looking converse must both be present. Also, both left-hand properties must be inside the biconditional. | ✅ `(NormalSpace X ∧ IsCountablyParacompact X) ↔ NormalSpace (X × Set.Icc (0:ℝ) 1)`. |
| 5 | Hypothesis placement | A candidate that assumes `[NormalSpace X]` as a typeclass and states only `IsCountablyParacompact X ↔ NormalSpace (X × I)` proves a strictly weaker theorem: the `←` direction then no longer has to recover normality of $X$ from normality of the product, which is part of Engelking's assertion. | ✅ `NormalSpace X` appears as a conjunct of the biconditional, not as an instance. ❗ Predicted error: promoting `NormalSpace X` to an instance binder. |
| 6 | Faithful encoding (the interval factor) | $I$ is the closed unit interval **as a topological space**, i.e. the subtype `↥(Set.Icc (0:ℝ) 1)` with the subspace topology, and the product carries the product topology. Replacing $I$ by $\mathbb{R}$ changes the theorem (normality of $X \times \mathbb{R}$ is not what Dowker characterizes); replacing it by `Set.Icc 0 1 : Set ℝ` in a `NormalSpace`-style claim about a *set* is a type error in disguise. | ✅ `NormalSpace (X × Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1)` uses the subtype coercion; mathlib's `unitInterval` is an `abbrev` for exactly `Set.Icc (0:ℝ) 1`, so this is literally the idiomatic `X × I`. ❗ Predicted error: `X × ℝ` or `X × (Icc 0 1 : Set ℝ)` misused. |
| 7 | Mathlib conventions / universes | Mathlib has neither countable paracompactness nor Dowker's theorem (checked), so the custom definition is justified; but `IsCountablyParacompact` quantifies its covers over `ι : Type v` for a free universe `v`, and the refinement index `κ` over the same `v`. | ⚠️ Acceptable. Since only countable covers occur, an equivalent and cleaner formulation indexes them by `ℕ` (or a `Countable` type in `Type u`), removing the universe parameter; candidates using `U : ℕ → Set X` should be accepted as faithful. |
